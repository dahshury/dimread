**Settings → Schedule** answers one question — *when is it night?* — then lists what
runs on that answer: the colour filter's day-to-night fade, and the Windows system
theme. Those were once two tabs with two sunrise pairs, which let DimRead hold two
opinions about dusk.

The on/off switch is not here. **Auto day/night** sits at the top of
**Settings → Display** next to the sliders because it takes effect instantly; this
tab holds the schedule behind it.

## Two profiles, not two modes

DimRead never switches to a "night mode". Each of the eight preset modes stores
**two** endpoints per axis — a day Kelvin/brightness pair and a night pair — and the
engine interpolates between them. `Health` is 5000 K at 90% by day and 3700 K at 80%
by night; `Reading` is 5500 K at 85% in both, so it looks the same whatever the clock
says. Full table on the [Display](/docs/display#the-eight-modes) page.

The schedule produces one number between 0 and 1 (1 = full day, 0 = full night) and
everything reads from it. A coarse phase label rides alongside it — **Day** at 0.999
and above, **Night** at 0.001 and below, **Transition** in between. The tray icon has
only two artworks, cool for day and warm for night, so a transition draws as day; the
tray tooltip carries the exact Kelvin and percentage instead.

**Note:** switching modes, a per-app rule, a hotkey or the tray flyout never
suspends the schedule. Only turning **Auto day/night** off does — and that pins the
output to the **day** profile, not to whatever was last on screen.

## Day & night settings

### Time source

Three segments: **Automatic** (coordinates from your system timezone), **Manual**
(you say where you are), **Custom time** (fixed clock hours, no astronomy). A fresh
install starts on **Automatic**, so the schedule follows real sun times for wherever
your machine says it is without you entering anything.

Picking **Custom time** leaves your location choice untouched, so coming back to a
sun-based schedule restores the way you had it set up.

### Location

Shown when Time source is **Manual**. Two segments — **Timezone** or **Coordinates**
— refining how you give your position. Defaults to **Timezone**, the option that
needs no outside knowledge.

### Your timezone

A searchable picker over the **418 canonical IANA zones** compiled into the app,
grouped by region and badged with a country code. Search matches city, country, code
and raw zone id, so `Berlin`, `Germany`, `DE` and `Europe/Berlin` all find the same
row. Empty by default, which falls back to detection. Sun times are computed for the
zone's reference city.

### Latitude and Longitude

Shown when Location is **Coordinates**. Latitude takes -90 to 90 degrees, longitude
-180 to 180, stepping by 1° — hold <Kbd>Alt</Kbd> for 0.1°. Both default to 0, which
is the Gulf of Guinea, so use this only if you know your coordinates.

### Sunrise and Sunset

**Custom time** only. Local clock times, 0-23 hours and 0-59 minutes, defaulting to
07:00 and 19:00. Sunrise is when the morning ramp starts; sunset is when full night
is reached.

### Detected location

A read-only readout, shown for every source except Custom time. Its sun times come
from the same backend function that drives the screen, so it cannot disagree with
what is being applied. It says one of:

- **"From your system timezone, Berlin."** — resolution worked, followed by
  *"Today: sunrise 05:12, sunset 21:26."*
- **"…has no known city. Pick another timezone, or enter coordinates."** — the
  platform named a zone with no locality, like `UTC` or `Etc/GMT+5`.
- **"Your system timezone could not be read…"** — the platform named nothing.
- **"Today the sun does not rise or set at this location."** — polar day or polar
  night. Midnight sun holds the day profile; polar night holds the night profile.

Every fallback is reported rather than silently scheduling for 0°, 0°.

### Transition

How many minutes the fade between the two profiles takes. Default **60**, range 0-240,
stepping by 15. It applies to all three time sources.

The ramp is asymmetric on purpose: the morning ramp **starts at sunrise** and climbs
to full day over the window, and the evening ramp **starts one window before sunset**
so full night lands exactly at sunset. Setting 0 gives no instant step — the window
is floored at one minute.

<Callout type="info">
**Transition** is not **Smooth transition**. This is the width of the day-to-night
ramp in minutes; the other, on the Display tab, is the roughly 360 ms ease applied to
any single change.
</Callout>

## Why there is no location permission prompt

DimRead never asks for location, never calls a geolocation API, and never makes a
network request for sun times. The coordinates come from the **system timezone**,
mapped through an IANA tzdb table generated into the binary — 418 zones plus 131
deprecated aliases, so a machine reporting `Asia/Calcutta` still resolves to Kolkata.

A zone's reference locality is by construction the place its civil clock is set for,
and sunrise moves only about 4 minutes per degree of longitude, so `Europe/Berlin` is
good to a few minutes anywhere in Germany. The sun maths is a port of the NOAA Solar
Calculator, accurate to about a minute at mid latitudes. Detection reads the registry
on Windows, `CFTimeZone` on macOS and `/etc/localtime` on Linux; on failure it says
so rather than guessing.

## Over midnight, after sleep, across DST

Nothing is cached. Every evaluation re-reads the clock and recomputes the sun times
with the DST-correct offset for that instant, so a date change, midnight and a DST
jump need no special case — the night plateau spans midnight naturally.

Between boundaries DimRead sleeps rather than polls: it wakes at the next sunrise,
ramp edge or sunset, and inside a ramp ticks every 1% of the transition window — 36
seconds at the default 60. Windows also listens for clock-change and power-resume
broadcasts. macOS and Linux have no native wall-clock-change notification, so the
schedule reconciles on resume, on window focus, or at its next deadline.

## Windows theme

### System theme

**Light**, **Dark**, **From day to night**, or **Disable**. Default **Disable**. It
writes the Windows *system* theme only — DimRead's own UI is permanently dark, and
the app-theme key that would re-skin every other app was removed on purpose. Some
apps ignore the setting entirely. Unlike the filter this is a hard switch, not a
ramp: light from sunrise until sunset, dark otherwise.

### Follow the day & night schedule

On by default, shown only when System theme is **From day to night**. It reuses the
boundaries above, resolved location and all, so the theme turns over on the same sun
times as the filter. Turn it off to give the theme its own hours.

**Note:** it borrows the times, not the master switch. Turning **Auto day/night** off
parks the colour filter on its day profile but leaves the theme flipping at sunrise
and sunset as before — the two are deliberately decoupled.

### System sunrise and System sunset

The opt-out pair, visible only when the follow toggle is off. `HH:MM`, defaulting to
07:00 and 19:00. This pair wraps midnight correctly when sunrise is later than sunset.

### Transparent taskbar

Applies an accent policy to the taskbar. Default off. It uses an undocumented Windows
API, best-effort, and the restore replays the policy captured before the first
override — if that capture failed, DimRead does nothing rather than pin the taskbar
to an opaque black bar.

<Callout type="warn">
The whole **Windows theme** section is Windows-only in effect. On macOS and Linux
the controls compile and persist but do nothing — no registry, no taskbar, no
theme change.
</Callout>

## Limitations

- **An inverted custom pair means permanent night.** A Custom time pair where sunset
  is earlier than sunrise (sunrise 19:00, sunset 07:00) gives the filter the night
  profile at every minute of the day — that ramp has no midnight-wrapping branch and
  the UI does not stop you. The Windows theme schedule *does* wrap, so the two
  visibly disagree there.
- **Mid-fade edits land on whichever endpoint dominates.** *Transition* is not an
  editable endpoint, so while the screen is showing a blend the hotkeys and the
  tray flyout write to the **day** value once the ramp is at least halfway to day
  (factor ≥ 0.5) and to the **night** value below that. Ties go to day. This is
  deliberate: editing the endpoint that is fading *out* would barely move the
  screen — at factor 0.05 a full-range brightness drag changes the output by 5 % —
  so an edit always has at least half the authority over what you can see. The
  consequence is that one hotkey press early in the evening ramp and another late
  in it can write to two different stored values.
- **No clock-change notification off Windows.** Change the system clock on macOS or
  Linux with no DimRead window focused and no resume happening, and the schedule
  catches up only at its next deadline, computed against the old clock.
- **Time fields are lenient, not validated.** Hours clamp to 0-23 and minutes to
  0-59; anything unparseable becomes 00:00.

Every edit here saves immediately — no Save button, no restart.
